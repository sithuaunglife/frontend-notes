# Forward Deployed Engineering

## Facts
- FDE Workflow:
 - 1. Listen to users - Understand what users say about their problems.
 - 2. Know what they want -	Identify the solution the client believes they need.
 - 3. Know what they should want - Identify the solution that actually addresses the underlying problem.
 - 4. Observe daily operations - Understand how people actually work, including their existing workflows and bottlenecks.
 - 5. Teach what already exists - Show users how existing tools and capabilities can solve their problems.
 - 6. Ship what doesn't exist yet - Build and deploy a solution when existing tools aren't sufficient.
- Client Communication Principles:
 - 1. Patience - Be patient when communicating with clients. They may struggle to explain technical requirements because they aren't developers. Listen carefully, ask clarifying questions, and avoid making assumptions.
 - 2. Keep a Client Preference Log - Record what your client likes, dislikes, and expects. This helps you avoid repeating mistakes and make better decisions throughout the project.
- Track software usage and relevant metrics to understand whether people are using the product and whether it delivers value.
- Owning every aspect of a project is exhausting. Learn to delegate tasks, automate repetitive work, and use existing tools instead of building everything from scratch.
- Building genuine professional relationships is an effective way to find your first client.
- Communicating clearly with clients and being reliable helps build trust, long-term relationships, and repeat business.
 - Why It Matters:
 - Communication: Listen to clients, understand their needs, and clarify expectations.
 - Trust: Be honest about your capabilities, progress, and limitations.
 - Reliability: Deliver quality work as agreed, meet deadlines, and communicate early when problems arise.
 - Retention: Clients who trust your work and communication are more likely to return for future projects.
 - Referrals: Satisfied clients may recommend your services to others.
- UI matters because first impressions influence how people perceive and interact with software.
